# Professional Website Content Synthesis

**Date:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Content direction concluded; implementation and final public-disclosure checks remain.  
**Scope:** Content and page architecture only. External visual references are intentionally excluded from this synthesis.

## 1. The main message

The website should help a visitor understand three things quickly:

1. Karim is an AI Engineer.
2. He builds reliable agents and knowledge systems for knowledge work.
3. He combines technical implementation with evaluation, observability, communication, and domain collaboration.

The central positioning is:

> **AI Engineer building reliable agents and knowledge systems for knowledge work.**

The plain-language explanation is:

> **I combine large language models with symbolic AI to turn complex domain knowledge into reliable, traceable software that people can inspect and improve.**

The broader story is:

> Karim builds AI systems that make complex expert knowledge more usable, measurable, and reviewable.

This message is broad enough to include legal/compliance, healthcare/scientific research, and other knowledge-intensive work. Legal and compliance should be the first professional proof domain, not the permanent identity of the entire site.

## 2. The visitor journey

The site should guide the visitor through a simple sequence:

```text
Who is Karim?
        ↓
What does he build?
        ↓
What has he done professionally?
        ↓
What technologies and methods does he use?
        ↓
How does he think and communicate?
        ↓
How can I learn more or get in touch?
```

The five main pages are:

- **About** — the person, trajectory, motivation, and working principles.
- **Projects** — selected personal, academic, open-source, and experimental work.
- **Experience** — professional roles and the systems built in those roles.
- **Skills** — capabilities first, technologies second.
- **Thoughts** — existing technical writing and learning in public.

The homepage is the entry point that connects these pages. Contact should initially be a call to action in the homepage, About page, Experience page, and footer rather than a separate content-heavy page.

## 9. Cross-page content rules

### Lead with capabilities, then technologies

The site should say what Karim can build and solve before listing tools.

### Make evidence local to the claim

Whenever possible, put the metric, repository, report, or article directly beside the project or experience claim it supports.

### Distinguish facts from interpretation

Safe established claims include:

- Builds agentic AI systems.
- Works with retrieval and knowledge graphs.
- Builds evaluation and observability infrastructure.
- Has experience in legal/compliance and healthcare/scientific AI.
- Has measured performance and efficiency improvements in named projects.

Interpretive claims should be demonstrated rather than stated as universal outcomes:

- Reliable agents
- Explainable AI
- Broad impact in regulated domains
- Clinical or legal authority

### Use limitations as a credibility signal

Each detailed case study should state what was not measured, what remained a prototype, or what could not be publicly disclosed.

### Keep the reader's language simple

Use “knowledge work,” “complex knowledge,” “reliable,” “traceable,” “observable,” and “reviewable” in public-facing copy. Introduce technical terms such as RAG, ReAct, CPT compression, and LLM-as-judge inside the relevant case studies.

## 11. The final reader takeaway

After reading the website, a technical visitor should be able to say:

> Karim is an early-career AI Engineer who builds and tests reliable agent, retrieval, and knowledge-graph systems for knowledge work. He has applied this approach to legal/compliance and healthcare/scientific work, can connect research ideas to working software, and communicates the engineering trade-offs clearly.
